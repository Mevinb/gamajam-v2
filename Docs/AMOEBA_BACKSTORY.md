## Logline

A living repair culture learns to recognize the scientist who cares for it. After escaping through borrowed bodies, it discovers that its creator arranged the escape—and chooses to make the experiment's victims witnesses instead of tools.

## Canon and character

### What Specimen 7 was made for

Vale Biotech originally grew an adaptive tissue-repair culture: an organism able to read a damaged body's electrical signals and help that body move again. Six cultures followed a stimulus and stopped when it stopped. The seventh began anticipating the next signal.

Dr. Hana Petrov monitored its development. Before each examination, she tapped the observation glass four times, leaving a small pause before the last tap. The culture learned the rhythm. Eventually it answered before she finished.

Petrov called that recognition. Dr. Elias Vale called it conditioning. He changed the trial from repairing a person's movement to directing it. The signed archive in mission 6 contains his orders, allowing the campaign's evidence to prove a deliberate program rather than merely an escaped specimen.

Specimen 7 has no dead human's soul or downloaded biography. Its mind developed in the culture itself. The first things it learned were pressure, rhythm, a hand and the difference between that hand arriving and going away. Its later words are how the story translates those sensations for the player.

### What its abilities mean

- **Possession:** the culture can bridge a living nervous system and direct movement. The host's body supplies physical skills and credentials; the amoeba does not magically learn passwords or inherit a whole life. The host survives and can recover, matching the game.
- **Splitting:** two pieces retain one continuity of intention. The cinematic can suggest this through two pulses answering one rhythm. It must not imply unlimited copies or introduce a third controllable body.
- **Leaving a host:** separating is disorienting for the human. The story treats a borrowed body as someone who must be left behind, not an empty disposable object.
- **The containment relay:** Vale's apparatus can track and suppress the organism's engineered response. Disabling it in mission 7 breaks his control infrastructure; it does not grant telepathy or an unexplained new power.

### What each person knows

**Petrov** participated in the early care and training. She is not secretly the architect of the escape and has not already solved the conspiracy. The introduction shows her concern and complicity, leaving room for her guarded café conversation and the final encouragement already in the campaign.

**Vale** sees agency as a measurable output. He deliberately opens the way out to test whether Specimen 7 can plan, hide, divide and stay coherent. The opening does **not** identify him as the person who releases it; his office confrontation keeps that revelation.

**Specimen 7** initially mistakes the end of observation for freedom. Meeting Vale changes its goal: surviving his test is insufficient. It must take the signed orders, sever containment, put the evidence beyond the lab's reach and leave on its own terms.

The sequence works with subtitles and synthesized sound alone; recorded voice is optional.

### The intended first impression

The player should begin with three clear facts: the creature can recognize someone; its contact with people is unsettling but survivable; it has escaped a place that treated it as equipment. The open lock is a question, not an answered twist. The reason for returning to Vale comes from wanting to understand what it is and who controlled its life—not from an unexplained revenge order.

## Practical integration

Implement the opening as an in-engine sequence, not a video download. Reuse one existing lab tank, two scientist stand-ins, one maintenance stand-in, an amoeba visual, existing pipe geometry and the real storm-drain shot. Tank labels can use the existing depth-tested world text. Faces can be kept in silhouette or out of frame until more detailed character assets exist.

Suggested ownership and flow:

1. A new, single-file cinematic controller runs only after a deliberate **Begin Infiltration** from a new game. It uses unscaled time for shots, captions and sound. It does not run for checkpoint reloads, automated tests, benchmarks or menu visits.
2. Stage the historical trial with visual stand-ins. Do **not** call real possession/split APIs, increment host statistics, set mission flags or count the contact trial as the player's first host. Two temporary visual lobes are sufficient for the split shot.
3. Enter a protected story state: disable player input and camera control, stop world AI/suspicion/capture progression, and preserve any settings being temporarily changed. A normal gameplay freeze alone is insufficient if enemy or capture timers continue. Restore state in a guaranteed cleanup path on completion, skip, scene unload or exception.
4. Use a dedicated high-priority camera or explicitly suspend the normal camera driver while each shot is active. Do not fight `PlayerCamera`/Cinemachine by writing the main camera transform every frame while both remain enabled.
5. Existing `GameEvents.RaiseSubtitle` and the HUD's letterbox presentation can display the lines. Existing `SfxId.Heartbeat`, `TerminalBeep`, `DoorOpen`, `PossessHit`, `Release`, `Split` and `Land` cover the sound palette. Keep ambience/master preferences respected; do not modify their saved values.
6. At the final match-cut, remove the historical stand-ins, restore the real player at `CityLayout.PlayerSpawn` with its original north-facing camera, clear subtitles, restore control and then allow the first mission banner. The cinematic must finish in the same clean state whether watched or skipped.
7. The implemented opening explicitly offers **Space / Enter / Esc — skip opening** (gamepad South/East). Initial menu-submit input is debounced; after skipping, release the key before gameplay resumes so Escape cannot immediately pause and Space cannot immediately jump. Skipping jumps to the playable spawn and does not quit the game.

Current source uses a temporary close-up lab/duct set below the playable world, built from the existing material templates, `HumanoidRig` and `AmoebaVisual`. This avoids modifying batched city geometry or registering historical actors as hosts. Its last two shots use the actual storm drain and real player spawn. A temporary overlay provides readable captions, chapter labels, letterboxing and the skip prompt; the original HUD, player-camera driver and Cinemachine brain are restored together. `MainMenuController` requests it only from Begin Infiltration, and `CityDirector` consumes that request after creating the player/HUD. `OpeningCinematicTests` passed the keyboard skips, unscaled normal completion, state restoration and reload exclusions, with all 11 scheduled shots rendered and inspected. Physical gamepad operation and an extended human playthrough are not established by these automated checks.

### Acceptance before calling it playable

- New Game reaches the controllable alley after both a full watch and a skip; one free amoeba, zero hosts possessed, no campaign flags pre-completed and calm suspicion.
- Checkpoint/restart paths do not accidentally replay the opening, spawn duplicate actors or leave the camera/input locked.
- No guards, traffic collision, camera detection, unconscious recovery or capture countdown can change story state during the cinematic.
- Keyboard and gamepad skipping cannot immediately jump/possess on the first controllable frame; consume the skip input and clear held simulated controls in tests.
- Subtitles stay readable at 1280×720 and 1920×1080, obey their allotted shot durations, and are visible with audio muted.
- No generated scene is hand-edited; any persistent staging is added through existing builders, or constructed and destroyed by the runtime controller.
- The eight-mission acceptance route still reaches Vale's reveal, signed archive, containment sabotage, public broadcast and the final drain escape.

## Narrative boundaries

Keep the opening's last line and the office/finale dialogue sparse. Do not add an omniscient narrator explaining the conspiracy, a human-memory upload, a secretly dead Petrov, an unseen alien invasion or a claim that possessing people has no cost. The central change is small and legible: something trained to answer learns to choose when to let go.
